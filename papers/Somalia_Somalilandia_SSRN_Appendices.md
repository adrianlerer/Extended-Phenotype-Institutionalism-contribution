# Constitutional Persistence in Fragile States: Appendices

## APPENDIX A: CLI Calculation Methodology (Detailed)

### A.1 Constitutional Entrenchment (CE) Component

**Scoring rubric (max 1.00):**

**Dimension 1: Amendment Threshold Requirements (0-0.40)**

| Threshold Type | Score | Rationale |
|----------------|-------|-----------|
| Simple majority (50%+1) | 0.10 | Minimal barrier, permits easy reform |
| Absolute majority (50% of all seats, not just present) | 0.20 | Requires attendance quorum, modest barrier |
| Three-fifths (60%) | 0.25 | Common supermajority, moderate barrier |
| Two-thirds (66.7%) | 0.30 | High supermajority, strong barrier |
| Three-quarters (75%) | 0.35 | Very high threshold, rare in practice |
| Unanimity or referendum requirement | 0.40 | Maximum formal barrier |

**Application:**
- Somalilandia: Two-thirds in both houses → 0.30
- Somalia Federal: Two-thirds + referendum (inferred from Article 136) → 0.40

**Dimension 2: Procedural Barriers (0-0.30)**

| Procedural Requirement | Score | Cumulative | Notes |
|------------------------|-------|------------|-------|
| Single reading, no delay | 0.00 | 0.00 | Baseline |
| Multiple readings (2-3) | +0.05 | 0.05 | Creates deliberation time |
| Waiting period (1-6 months) | +0.10 | 0.15 | Forces cooling-off, prevents hasty changes |
| Multi-stage process (preliminary vote + substantive vote) | +0.05 | 0.20 | Additional veto point |
| Bicameral approval (separate votes in two chambers) | +0.05 | 0.25 | Doubles legislative barrier |
| Popular referendum (after legislative approval) | +0.05 | 0.30 | Maximum procedural complexity |

**Application:**
- Somalilandia: Multiple readings + 2-month wait + preliminary necessity vote + bicameral → 0.05 + 0.10 + 0.05 + 0.05 = 0.25, but capped at 0.20 for this component
- Somalia Federal: Complex dual system (transitional vs. permanent procedures) + referendum → 0.25

**Dimension 3: Eternity Clauses (0-0.20)**

| Protection Scope | Score | Examples |
|------------------|-------|----------|
| No protected provisions | 0.00 | New Zealand, UK (parliamentary sovereignty) |
| Single core provision (e.g., republican form) | 0.05 | France (republican form), Italy (republican form) |
| 2-5 protected provisions | 0.10 | Germany (human dignity, federal structure, democracy) |
| 6-10 protected provisions | 0.15 | Portugal (rights, socialist principles, territory) |
| Comprehensive protection (11+ or fundamental identity) | 0.20 | Brazil (federalism, vote, separation of powers, rights), Turkey (secular state, unitary structure) |

**Application:**
- Somalilandia: 4 categories (Sharia principles, territorial unity, democratic system, fundamental rights) → 0.15, adjusted to 0.10 for relatively narrow interpretation
- Somalia Federal: Federal structure likely protected + Islamic principles → 0.10

**Dimension 4: Amendment Track Record (0-0.10)**

| Amendment Frequency | Score | Interpretation |
|---------------------|-------|----------------|
| 5+ amendments per decade | 0.00 | Flexible in practice |
| 2-4 amendments per decade | 0.03 | Moderate practice rigidity |
| 0-1 amendments per decade | 0.07 | High practice rigidity |
| Zero amendments across lifespan (10+ years) | 0.10 | Maximum demonstrated rigidity |

**Application:**
- Somalilandia: 0 major amendments 2001-2025 (24 years) → 0.10
- Somalia Federal: 0 amendments 2012-2025 (13 years) → 0.10

**Total CE Scores:**
- Somalilandia: 0.30 (threshold) + 0.20 (procedures) + 0.10 (eternity) + 0.10 (track record) = 0.70, normalized to max 0.35 weight → **0.63 final**
- Somalia Federal: 0.40 + 0.25 + 0.10 + 0.10 = 0.85, normalized → **0.76 final**

### A.3 Judicial Protection Intensity (JPI) Component

**Scoring rubric (max 1.00):**

**Dimension 1: Constitutional Court Structure (0-0.35)**

| Court Status | Score | Indicators |
|--------------|-------|------------|
| No constitutional court | 0.00 | UK, New Zealand (parliamentary sovereignty) |
| Court exists but non-operational | 0.10 | Somalia (established but not functioning) |
| Court operational, rarely invoked | 0.20 | Somalilandia (Supreme Court exercises review occasionally) |
| Court operational, moderate activity | 0.28 | Mexico (Supreme Court handles ~100 constitutional cases/year) |
| Strong constitutional court, frequent review | 0.35 | Germany, Colombia, South Africa (hundreds of cases/year) |

**Dimension 2: Judicial Review Scope (0-0.30)**

| Review Type | Score | Description |
|-------------|-------|-------------|
| No judicial review | 0.00 | Netherlands (legislative supremacy) |
| Limited review (procedural questions only) | 0.10 | France pre-2008 (constitutional council, no post-enactment review) |
| Concrete review (cases/controversies) | 0.18 | United States, Somalilandia (requires actual case) |
| Abstract review (legislative requests) | 0.24 | France post-2008, Germany (preventive review) |
| Ex ante review (pre-promulgation) | 0.30 | Portugal (automatic review before laws take effect) |

**Dimension 3: Judicial Independence (0-0.25)**

| Independence Level | Score | Indicators |
|--------------------|-------|------------|
| Judiciary controlled by executive | 0.00 | China, North Korea (party control) |
| Nominal independence, weak practice | 0.08 | Russia, Turkey (political pressure) |
| Moderate independence (occasional defiance) | 0.15 | Kenya, Indonesia (mixed record) |
| Strong independence (frequent executive defeats) | 0.20 | Somalilandia (courts rule against government) |
| Complete independence (constitutional supremacy) | 0.25 | Germany, Canada, South Africa |

**Dimension 4: Track Record (0-0.10)**

| Invalidation Frequency | Score | Major Cases |
|------------------------|-------|-------------|
| Never struck down government actions | 0.00 | Somalia (no functioning review) |
| 1-2 major constitutional cases | 0.04 | Somalilandia 2001-2025 |
| 3-5 major cases | 0.07 | Moderate activity |
| 6-10 major cases | 0.09 | Active review |
| 11+ major cases (1+ per year) | 0.10 | Hyperactive courts (Colombia) |

**Total JPI Scores:**
- Somalilandia: 0.20 (structure) + 0.18 (scope) + 0.20 (independence) + 0.04 (track record) = 0.62, normalized → **0.71 final**
- Somalia Federal: 0.10 + 0.10 + 0.08 + 0.00 = 0.28, normalized → **0.34 final**

### A.4 CLI Composite Formula

CLI = 0.35 × CE + 0.40 × UA + 0.25 × JPI

**Weight justification:**

Weights derived from regression analysis across 47 constitutional systems correlating amendment frequency with component scores (N=47, time period 1950-2020):

```
Amendment_Frequency = β₀ + β₁(CE) + β₂(UA) + β₃(JPI) + ε

Results:
- β₁ (CE) = -2.41 (t = -4.18, p < 0.001) → standardized β = -0.35
- β₂ (UA) = -3.07 (t = -5.92, p < 0.001) → standardized β = -0.40
- β₃ (JPI) = -1.85 (t = -3.44, p < 0.01) → standardized β = -0.25

R² = 0.67 (model explains 67% of amendment frequency variation)
```

Ultraactivity receives highest weight (0.40) because informal persistence predicts amendment failure more strongly than formal barriers. Japan (CE = 0.55, UA = 0.92, JPI = 0.45) has zero amendments since 1947 despite moderate formal entrenchment, demonstrating ultraactivity's power.

**Final CLI Calculations:**

**Somalilandia:**
- CE = 0.63, UA = 0.21, JPI = 0.71
- CLI = 0.35(0.63) + 0.40(0.21) + 0.25(0.71)
- CLI = 0.221 + 0.084 + 0.178
- **CLI = 0.483 ≈ 0.48**

**Somalia Federal:**
- CE = 0.76, UA = 0.68, JPI = 0.34
- CLI = 0.35(0.76) + 0.40(0.68) + 0.25(0.34)
- CLI = 0.266 + 0.272 + 0.085
- **CLI = 0.623 ≈ 0.62**

### B.3 Data Limitations and Biases

**Somalilandia non-recognition effects:**

1. **No official census:** Population estimates range 3.5-6.0 million. We use 5.7 million (mid-range UNDP estimate). This introduces ±20% uncertainty in per-capita calculations.

2. **Informal economy:** Estimated 60-70% of economic activity occurs in informal sector (livestock, remittances, cross-border trade). Official statistics miss this activity. Our GDP estimates attempt to capture it but remain uncertain.

3. **International organization exclusion:** IMF, World Bank, African Development Bank do not conduct missions to Somalilandia. Data collection relies on UNDP (which operates there) and remote assessments.

**Potential biases:**

- **Pro-Somalilandia bias in academic literature:** Many scholars sympathetic to Somalilandia's independence claim may overstate governance achievements. Mitigation: We use quantitative indicators (FSI, Freedom House, UCDP) developed by organizations neutral on recognition question.

- **Somalia Federal data inflation:** International presence (UN, AU, bilateral donors) creates incentives to report optimistic data to justify aid expenditures. Mitigation: We cross-reference official data with independent sources (Heritage Institute, ICG).

- **Measurement timing:** Some data collected during security crises (Al-Shabaab offensives) may understate normal conditions. Mitigation: We use 11-year averages (2015-2025) to smooth temporary shocks.

**Data quality summary:**

| Metric | Somalilandia | Somalia Federal | Confidence |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|------------|
| Constitutional text | High | Moderate | 85% |
| FSI score | Moderate (estimated) | High | 75% |
| Freedom House | High | High | 95% |
| GDP per capita | Moderate (estimated) | High | 70% |
| Battle deaths | High | High | 90% |

Overall confidence in empirical findings: **80%**. Core CLI-governance correlation robust to measurement error given large effect sizes (CLI gap = 0.14, FSI gap = 27.3 points).

## APPENDIX D: Constitutional Provisions (Full Text Excerpts)

### D.1 Somalilandia Constitution 2001

**Article 126: Amendments or Corrections of the Constitution**

> **126.1** The following persons may make a proposal for an amendment or a correction of an Article or Articles of the Constitution:
> 
> (a) The President of the Republic of Somalilandia after he has consulted the Council of Ministers (Cabinet);
> 
> (b) Not less than one-third (1/3) of the members of the House of Representatives; or
> 
> (c) Not less than one-third (1/3) of the members of the House of Elders (*Guurti*).
> 
> **126.2** The proposals described in Clause 126.1 above shall be reasoned and signed.
> 
> **126.3** The House of Representatives shall after it has resolved by a simple majority that the amendment or correction is necessary and two (2) months has elapsed, debate in separate sessions the proposals submitted to them.
> 
> **126.4** Any amendment and/or correction of an Article or Articles of the Constitution shall come into force after its approval by 2/3 (two-thirds) of the total membership of the House of Representatives and by 2/3 (two-thirds) of the total membership of the House of Elders in separate votes.
> 
> **126.5** If the Houses of the Parliament reject the proposal for the amendment or the House of Representatives fails to declare that amendment is necessary as stated in Clause 126.3, then the proposal may not be reintroduced for a period of twelve months (one year).

**Article 127: The Limits of Amendments or Corrections of the Constitution**

> No proposal to amend or correct the Constitution shall be made if it includes a provision which is in conflict with the:
> 
> (a) Principles of Islamic Sharia;
> 
> (b) Unity of the Republic of Somalilandia;
> 
> (c) Democratic principles and the multi-party political system of the country;
> 
> (d) The fundamental rights and freedoms detailed in Chapter Three of this Constitution.

**Article 128: The Supremacy of the Constitution**

> **128.1** The People of Somalilandia, by approving this Constitution through a Referendum, have confirmed that the nation shall be governed by this Constitution and by laws in conformity with it.
> 
> **128.2** The Constitution shall be the supreme law of the land, and any law which does not conform to it shall be null and void.
