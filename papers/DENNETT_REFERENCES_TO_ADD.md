# Dennett Bibliography to Add to CriptoIus Paper

## Primary Dennett Sources

### Dennett, D. (2003). *La Evolución de la Libertad*. Barcelona: Paidós.
**English Original:** Dennett, D. (2003). *Freedom Evolves*. New York: Viking Press.
- **Chapter 6**: "Memes como Simbiontes Culturales" (pp. 202-204) - Parasitic, Commensal, Mutualistic memes
- **Chapter 9**: "Auparse a la Libertad" (pp. 292-313) - Bootstrapping, voluntary self-binding, Ulysses contracts
- **Key Quote (p. 298)**: "La ingeniería memética es una innovación muy reciente... algunos de sus primeros y más célebres productos fueron La República de Platón y la Política de Aristóteles."
- **Key Quote (p. 215)**: "No es una cuestión de memes contra razones. Ni siquiera de memes contra buenas razones."
- **Key Quote (p. 305)**: "We are the first species that can have lives shaped as much by the memes we acquire as by the genes we inherit."

### Dennett, D. (1995). *Darwin's Dangerous Idea: Evolution and the Meanings of Life*. New York: Simon & Schuster.
- **Chapters 11-12**: Cultural evolution, memes as replicators
- **Chapter 13**: Universal Darwinism - selectors without knowledge
- **Key insight**: Selection pressure operates in cultural domains (including law) analogously to biological evolution

### Dennett, D. (1984). *Elbow Room: The Varieties of Free Will Worth Wanting*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
- **Chapter 6**: Compatibilist freedom - choosing determinants vs escaping determination
- **Key Quote (p. 169)**: Test for freedom: "Could agent have done otherwise, holding reasons constant?"
- **Application to CriptoIus**: Voluntary precedent adoption = reason-responsive choice

## Complementary Sources (Cited by Dennett)

### Skyrms, B. (1996). *Evolution of the Social Contract*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
- **Chapters 1-3**: Cake-cutting game, polymorphic ESSs
- **Key Model**: How "modestos ilustrados" recognize exploitation and adopt efficient strategies
- **Application**: Parties adopting high-JurisRank precedents as rational coordination
- **Cited by Dennett** (Freedom Evolves, pp. 294-297)

### Gibbard, A. (1990). *Wise Choices, Apt Feelings: A Theory of Normative Judgment*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.
- **Chapter 11**: "Ingeniería psíquica" - designing norms that align with human motivations
- **Key Concept**: Normas moderadas vs imperativas (moderate vs imperative norms)
- **Application**: CriptoIus precedents as moderate norms (voluntary, incentive-compatible)
- **Cited by Dennett** (Freedom Evolves, pp. 299-313)

### Boyd, R., & Richerson, P. J. (1992). "Punishment Allows the Evolution of Cooperation (or Anything Else) in Sizable Groups." *Ethology and Sociobiology*, 13(3), 171-195.
- **Key Finding**: Cultural punishment mechanisms enable group cooperation
- **Application**: RootFinder + fairness scoring as mechanisms to punish parasitic precedents
- **Cited by Dennett** (Freedom Evolves, Chapter 7)

## Additional Relevant Dennett Works

### Dennett, D. (2017). *From Bacteria to Bach and Back: The Evolution of Minds*. New York: W. W. Norton.
- **Chapters 8-9**: Competence without comprehension, free-floating rationales
- **Application**: Precedents spread because they work, not because users understand why
- **Key Quote (Chapter 9)**: "Memes are thinking tools... Forming part of thought is how a meme proves itself."
